Flooding

A valley built for water, and a town built in it

Historic Ellicott City sits where five streams converge before reaching the Patapsco. The July 2016 and May 2018 flash floods moved cars and collapsed buildings on Main Street in under two hours.

Extreme heat

Some neighborhoods run hotter than others, by design

Parking lots, warehouse roofs, and thin tree canopy along Route 1 and around Wilde Lake Village Center trap heat well past sundown — a pattern the county can map block by block.

Stormwater & water quality

Where the rain goes matters as much as how much falls

Impervious surface across Columbia and the Route 1 corridor sends stormwater straight into streams that feed the Patapsco, carrying road salt, sediment, and runoff with it.

Why this site exists

Local data, read plainly

Howard County already publishes serious climate and flood data through Climate Forward and its open-data GIS portal. Most residents never see it, because dashboards built for planners aren’t built for a resident trying to figure out whether their street floods or their block runs hot. Environmento exists to translate that public data into something you can read in five minutes and use — not to replace the official tools, but to point you toward them with context attached.
